Recognize the material

Explore the types of waste Smart Waste can help identify.

A photo gives us a starting point. The item’s condition, material mix, and local collection options help decide what happens next.

♳

Plastic

Examples: Drink bottles, detergent containers, yogurt tubs, shopping bags, and wrappers.

What matters: Rigid and flexible plastics are handled differently. Emptying and rinsing containers can reduce contamination.

Typical next step: Check the resin and accepted shapes with your local recycling service. Keep soft film out of curbside bins unless they allow it.

▤

Paper & cardboard

Examples: Shipping boxes, newspapers, mail, paperboard packaging, and clean paper bags.

What matters: Paper is easiest to recover when it is clean and dry. Grease and food residue can affect whether it is recyclable.

Typical next step: Flatten clean boxes and separate greasy sections. Check whether coated cartons belong in a different stream.

♢

Glass

Examples: Beverage bottles, food jars, and broken glass pieces.

What matters: Broken glass needs safe handling. Drinking glasses, mirrors, and ceramics are not the same as glass packaging.

Typical next step: Empty bottles and jars and use your area’s glass collection route. Wrap shards securely and keep them apart.

▣

Metal

Examples: Aluminum drink cans, steel food cans, and metal lids.

What matters: Food residue should be removed, and local programs may have different rules for foil or composite packaging.

Typical next step: Empty and rinse common cans, then place them with metals where your service accepts them.

❋

Organic

Examples: Fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, tea leaves, and garden trimmings.

What matters: Home composters and municipal organics programs accept different materials. Meat and dairy need special attention.

Typical next step: Use a compost or food-scrap service that accepts the item. Remove stickers and packaging first.

⌁

E-waste

Examples: Phones, chargers, batteries, cables, headphones, and small appliances.

What matters: Electronics and batteries should not be placed in ordinary recycling or trash where dedicated collection is available.

Typical next step: Find a battery or e-waste collection point. Keep damaged batteries isolated and ask the collection service how to transport them.

◌

Other & mixed materials

Examples: Clothing, shoes, ceramics, multilayer wrappers, and objects made from several materials.

What matters: Composite items can be hard to sort from an image alone. Reuse or repair may be the most useful first option.

Typical next step: Separate parts when practical, donate usable goods, and check specialist drop-off guidance for the rest.
