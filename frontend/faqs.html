Helpful details before you scan

Questions, answered plainly.

Here is what Smart Waste can help with, how to read a result, and where local collection guidance still matters.

Smart Waste helps you identify an everyday item from a photo and understand a sensible next step for disposal. It explains the suggestion and flags when local rules or an uncertain image could change the answer.

A vision model looks at the image and estimates the item, material, and visible condition. A separate rule engine uses those details to select disposal guidance. The AI does not know your municipality’s current collection list unless you check it yourself.

You can try common plastic, paper, glass, metal, organic, electronic, textile, and mixed-material items. Smart Waste works best with one item in clear view. It may not recognize every product or every material from a photo.

The result should show a low-confidence notice and avoid presenting a guess as certain. Try another photo with better light and a simpler background. If it is still unclear, check the packaging label or ask your local waste service.

Food, liquid, damage, or contamination can change how an item should be handled. A clean bottle may be recyclable where a heavily soiled container is not. The scan only describes condition visible in the image, not what is hidden inside.

A home curbside bin, a public station, and a specialist drop-off can have different options. Context helps frame the guidance, but it does not tell us the exact rules for your town or building.

Use it as general guidance, then confirm local acceptance when it matters. Recognition can be imperfect, packaging can combine materials, and a municipality’s current instructions take priority. For batteries or hazardous items, use a designated service.

Yes. Collection equipment, contracts, and drop-off services differ between places, so the same material may be accepted in one area and refused in another. Check your municipality or waste provider’s latest list before sorting an unusual item.

Use a bright, in-focus photo of one item, with the whole object visible against a simple background. JPG, PNG, and WEBP images up to 8 MB are accepted. Avoid including people or private documents in the frame.

The application sends your image to its configured vision service to complete the requested analysis, then discards its in-memory copy when the request finishes. Smart Waste does not save images. The configured provider may process request data under its own terms, so review that provider’s privacy information before using the service.
